Held FILL, and what a Rest does to notes already sounding

A Fill is a short change that ends on a phrase line. Rest makes some lanes or parts play nothing (the kick drop). Swap makes drum lanes play a fill pattern instead (snare fills, tom runs, Dub's return fill). FILL on the Deluge is momentary: the fill plays while you hold it.

Question 1, by feel: what should a held FILL play? Either one entry the genre names (Techno: the kick drop, Dub: the return fill) every time, or a fresh seeded pick from the genre's fill weights on each press. Predictable or varied?

Question 2, by ear: when a Rest starts, what happens to a note that's already sounding (a long Bass note, a Pad chord, an open-hat tail)? Either it rings to its normal end and only new notes stop, or it's cut when the Rest starts. For a pitched part, cut means a note-off, so the sound's release still plays. For a drum lane, a note-off does nothing: kit samples under 2 s load as one-shots (ONCE), so cutting one means a fast choke, like the closed hat cutting the open hat. Does the answer differ between drums and pitched parts, or between a short automatic Rest and a long held one?
