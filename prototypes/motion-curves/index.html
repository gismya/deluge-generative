How Motion scales Groove length, fills and drop-outs

Motion (0–50) sets how fast the music changes. Each thing it speeds up has a value at Motion 25 that the genre gives: the Groove length, the fill chance, the drop-out chance, and so on. Today the spec scales all of them rate-linearly: Motion 0 turns the rate off and Motion 50 doubles it. For the Groove that's phrases = ceil(b × 25 / M), so the tail at low Motion is long. Techno with b = 6 is 1.5 min at Motion 25, 7.4 min at 5 and 37 min at 1.

The question: which curve should each rate use? The candidates are (a) rate-linear, (b) rate-linear with a floor (the Groove is never longer than k × b, so the rate never drops below 1/k of its Motion 25 value until Motion 0), (c) exponential (the rate doubles every 25 / log₂R Motion; R = 4 halves or doubles every 12.5), (d) exponential from a start point (the rate just above Motion 0 is "start" × the Motion 25 rate; start 0 slides to 0 with no jump, start 1/R is the same as (c)), and square (PERF-1's mutation curve). With every curve, Motion 0 means "never". Judge them on the timeline (each row is the same seed with one rate's curve swapped) and by ear (click a row to play it from there).

Fixed already: the Groove length is re-checked against the live Motion at every phrase line (the Groove ends at the first line where phrases played × rate(M) ≥ b), so a Motion turn takes effect inside a Groove. The sounds are rough browser stand-ins. Use headphones.
