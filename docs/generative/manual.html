Generative Mode User Manual

Play evolving, genre-aware music live on your Deluge, steer its energy and structure in real time, and turn any jam or Recipe into an editable Deluge song.

OLED Deluge only 8 built-in genres Perform · Compose · Capture 4/4 time

1. What Is Generative Mode?

Generative mode adds a built-in music generator to the Deluge. You give it a Recipe—a genre, tempo, key, scale, four macro settings, and a set of sounds—and it writes drums, bass, chords, pads, leads, and FX on the fly.

One engine drives two core modes—Perform and Compose—supported by Capture and Recipe files:

Perform

Plays live and non-destructively in a song of its own. Steer macros, force breakdowns and drops, mute parts, swap sounds, and switch genres mid-set.

Compose

Renders a fixed Recipe into a normal, editable Deluge song—complete with clips, arranger layout, and thinned automation ready to tweak in stock views.

Capture

Watches everything you do in Perform. When you leave, it replays your favourite pieces into regular Deluge songs saved on your SD card.

Recipe Files

Save and load Recipes in RECIPES/ on your SD card so you can come back to a groove or share it with another player.

Hardware & Scope Notes
  • OLED only: Generative mode requires an OLED Deluge. 7-segment displays are not supported.
  • 4/4 time: All built-in genres play in 4/4 on a 16th-note grid.
  • Internal sounds only in v1: Generative mode plays internal synths and SD card samples. MIDI/CV note output to external gear and playing along from an external MIDI keyboard are not in v1 (external notes are ignored in Perform).

2. Getting In and Out

Before your first session, unzip the Generative pack onto your SD card so all built-in genres have their custom presets, risers, impacts, and percussion.

Entering Generative Mode

  1. Go to Session view. (If you are in Arranger view, press SONG first—Arranger keeps its stock SHIFT+SONG shortcut.)
  2. Press SHIFT+SONG. The GENERATIVE menu opens while your current song keeps playing.
  3. Pick PERFORM or COMPOSE with SELECT.
  4. Choose where to start from in PERFORM FROM or COMPOSE FROM:
    • CURRENT RECIPE — appears once you have used Generative mode since power-on. Picks up right where you left off.
    • GENRE ▸ — picks a built-in genre and rolls a fresh Recipe (tempo, key, scale, sounds, and a new seed). Opens on your Current Recipe's genre, or Acid Techno before one exists.
    • FILE... — opens the Recipe browser on RECIPES/.

Replacing the Current Song

Before replacing whatever song you have open, Generative mode asks DISCARD SONG? → OK. Press BACK to cancel with nothing changed.

  • Perform asks right after you pick a source. While Perform loads its sounds, your old song keeps playing. Once loaded, Perform swaps in stopped on the SONG page.
  • Compose opens the Compose screen without discarding your song—picking GENRE ▸ or FILE... reads the SD card first under the working animation (BACK cancels with the Recipe unchanged). Compose only asks DISCARD SONG? when you press BUILD.

Starting & Stopping Playback in Perform

  • Press PLAY to start from bar 1 with the genre's Intro.
  • Press SHIFT+PLAY to skip the Intro and jump straight into a Groove.
  • Press PLAY again to stop immediately (tails ring out). Stopping drops every queued action—including any queued key change—and every start begins a fresh piece.

Leaving Perform

Hold BACK while in Perform. Music keeps playing while the Leave menu opens: choose which pieces to keep as normal songs via Capture, pick NOTHING (or LEAVE if nothing played) to exit to a blank session song, or pick CANCEL (or tap BACK) to keep playing.

Your Current Recipe stays in memory after you leave Perform or Compose until you switch the Deluge off. Save it with SAVE if you want to keep it across power cycles.

3. Genres & Subgenres

A genre is a built-in style definition. It sets the parts (4 to 6 instruments), tempo range, scales, swing, rhythm patterns, chord habits, Arc rules, and sound palette. You cannot edit a genre itself, though you can steer any Recipe it produces.

Some genres are grouped into subgenres under a parent menu row (such as TECHNO ▸). Moving between two subgenres—say from Acid Techno to Detroit Techno—is a full genre change with a transition.

How Each Genre Feels

Techno — Acid, Melodic, Detroit & Dub

TECHNO ▸ · Straight time · 8-bar phrases

All four Techno subgenres share four-on-the-floor kicks, offbeat hats, no claps on 2 and 4, Builds whose length and shape the BUILD set picks (see below), and kick-and-bass rest fills.

  • Acid Techno: Hypnotic and raw. Stays on one chord throughout while a mutating acid bassline sweeps its filter over 8 bars beneath a quiet root-and-fifth drone. Breaks drop down to the drone, kick, rim, and closed hat.
  • Melodic Techno: Driving and spacious. A rolling 16th-note bass leaves room for the kick while chords move every 2 bars in a minor key, topped by a sweeping 16th-note arpeggio. Breaks strip back to the pad, arp, and closed hat.
  • Detroit Techno: Warm and chord-led. Every chord carries an Extension (usually a 7th), adding a second (often a 9th) as Complexity rises. Syncopated stabs and warm strings drive the groove, and a motif lead enters automatically on Drops or when you raise Density.
  • Dub Techno: Deep and cavernous (115–125 BPM). One minor 7th/9th chord echoes through a dotted-8th ping-pong delay with 2-bar stab drop-outs, a long-note sub bass, shaker, and a low pedal pad. (Not to be confused with Roots Dub.)

Variant sets:

  • BUILD (all four subgenres) — ROLL (8/16-bar roll), LONG (16/32-bar, 2-bar kick cut), NO ROLL (8-bar, no roll), or HARD (4/8-bar, no Gap).
  • DRUMS Live (Acid & Detroit) — OFFBEAT, RUNNING (16th closed hats), PUMP (open hat on every &), or TRIBAL (busier rim & toms).
  • ACID Live (Acid) — CLASSIC, TALKING (busier line with ♭2, wider leaps & slides), or HYPNOTIC (root/octave, slow mutation).
  • ARP Live (Melodic) — RISING, PULSE (alternating root/octave 16ths), DOWN, or SPARSE (dotted-8th onsets).

House

HOUSE · 120–126 BPM · Swung 16ths

Punchy four-on-the-floor kick with claps anchored on beats 2 and 4, late-leaning hats, 7th/9th chord stabs, warm strings, and a motif lead that answers the bass on Builds and Drops. Groove drop-outs periodically pull out the stabs, strings, kick, or hats for 4–8 bars.

Variant sets:

  • LOOPS Live — how often Drops switch to a new chord loop: 1 IN 3, 1 IN 2, EVERY, or NEVER.
  • BUILD — build style: FULL (snare roll), NO LEAD, NO KICK (bass enters on last bar), or FILTER (no roll, riser, or gap; kick out and filtered bass leads in).
  • BASS — RIFF (3-3-2 groove), OFFBEAT, OFF ROOT (root-only at low Complexity), or PICKUPS (offbeat with 16th pickups).
  • CHORDS — LOOP (moving chord progression) or VAMP (two-chord i–iv vamp).

Roots Dub

ROOTS DUB · 70–80 BPM · Dotted-8th echoes & Siren

Slow, heavy, and spacious. A pentatonic bassline carries the melody while an offbeat skank and a breathing melodica bounce through dotted-8th analog echo. Frequent drop-outs pull instruments out while throwing their last hit into reverb and delay, and a dub Siren sweeps on Drops (and on Breaks at higher Density).

Variant sets:

  • SKANK Live — 7THS or PLAIN (triads).
  • DRUMS Live · Drop-changing — ONE DROP, ROCKERS, or STEPPERS. Can automatically switch at Drops (40% chance per Drop) unless you lock it to one variant.
  • BREAK — 7 BARS (1-phrase Break minus the 1-bar Build) or 15 BARS (2-phrase Break minus the 1-bar Build).
  • MIX Live — drop-out style: VERSION (balanced), TUBBY (heavy, frequent cuts), or LIGHT (mostly 1-bar spot echoes).
  • BASS Live — BURSTS (spaced phrases), ROLLING (8th-note lines), or HEAVY (sparse, long root/5th notes).

Psytrance — Classic Psytrance

PSYTRANCE ▸ CLASSIC · 138–146 BPM · 16-bar phrases

Fast and relentless. A tight rolling 16th bass carves around every kick hit over a root-and-fifth pedal drone with no chord changes. A filter-swept arp climbs through 8-, 16-, or 32-bar builds (cutting kick and bass on the last 4 bars) into hard drops whose lift decays over 4 phrases, bringing the sweeping lead in for the first two phrases.

Variant sets:

  • FIGURE Live — Arp style: SEQUENCE (16th loop), WALK (Up / Up-down arp), SHUFFLE (the same walks in random order), or TRIAD (walk without 7ths).
  • BREAK — ARP (drone, arp, closed hat), LEAD (drone, lead, hat), or NO HAT (drone and lead alone).
  • ARP+LEAD Live — how the Arp behaves when the Lead plays: DROP BOTH (both full on Drops, Arp 8ths elsewhere), TURNS (Arp rests), or 8THS (Arp plays 8ths everywhere).
  • LEAD — when the Lead enters: DROPS (Drops only), GROOVES (every Groove), or INTRO (late Intro and Breaks too).
  • ACCENTS Live — where Arp octave jumps land: ANYWHERE, 3-3-2, or OFFBEAT.

Ambient

AMBIENT · 60–90 BPM · Fades through · No heard drop

Slow, wide, and gentle. Open-voiced pads hold common tones across 8-bar+ chord changes so harmonies blur smoothly instead of re-striking. A deep drone anchors the bottom, and sparse bells chime above. At default Density (25), soft percussion is silent in Grooves and Breaks, with only mallets and pings entering softly late in Builds and fading in Drops; raising Density from 29 to 47 brings all six soft percussion lanes (mallets, wood, hats, rim, brushes, kick) into the Groove one by one. Builds swell softly with no roll, no gap, and no drop shock. Transitions into Ambient default to FADE.

Variant sets:

  • COLOUR Live — chord colour: ADD9 (9ths & sus4), PURE (plain triads), or LUSH 7/9 (rich 7th + 9th chords).
  • MOTION — harmonic pace: DRIFT (balanced 8-bar chords), STILL (very slow 16-bar chords near home), or WANDER (wider chord moves).

4. Recipe, Seed, Entropy & Takes

Five simple ideas explain why Generative mode stays repeatable while still feeling alive:

  • Recipe: The musical blueprint. It stores the genre, tempo, key, scale (one of 12 supported seven-note scales: Major, Minor, Dorian, Phrygian, Lydian, Mixolydian, Locrian, Melodic Minor, Harmonic Minor, Hungarian Minor, Marva, and Arabian), swing, the four macro values, any per-part octave shifts and Part biases, the chosen sound files, and the variant picks. It holds no notes.
  • Current Recipe: The one active Recipe held in RAM while your Deluge is on. Perform steers it and Compose edits it, so switching between Perform and Compose always keeps your latest settings.
  • Seed: A single number sitting beside the Recipe, shown read-only as a 7-character code like 1A2B3C4 (using characters that never look alike on the OLED). The seed fixes every musical choice: patterns, melodies, chord walks, fills, drop-outs, section lengths, and drift. Play the same Recipe and seed with the same moves, and you hear the exact same notes.
  • Fresh entropy: True randomness used only when you explicitly roll something new—picking a genre, running Regenerate, or rolling a new sound. It picks tempo, key, scale, swing, sounds, variant picks, and a new seed. Because those results are saved into the Recipe, drawing a NEW SEED (in Compose) creates brand-new patterns and melodies while keeping your tempo, key, sounds, and variants intact.
  • Takes (1–16 per part): Each part has its own take counter. Re-rolling a part's pattern advances its take, giving that part new rhythms and lines without disturbing any other part. You can step backwards to any earlier take at any time—the old pattern comes right back. Drawing a new seed, Regenerating, or changing genre resets every part to take 1.
How to See Your Seed Code

In Perform, push the Y (▲▼) encoder at any time to pop up the current key, scale, and seed code (for example, A MINOR 1A2B3C4). You can also see it as the title of SEED + TAKES ▸ in the Perform menu and on the Compose screen.

5. The Four Macros & Part Bias

Four global macros, each running from 0 to 50 (starting at 25 in built-in genres), shape the whole performance in real time. Each macro pushes the music in the same direction across every genre:

Macro What It Controls Low (0–15) High (35–50)
Energy (E) Intensity: note velocity and sound shading (brighter, shorter decay, more drive, drier). Does not change how many notes play. Soft, dark, long, and wet Hard, bright, tight, driven, and punchy
Density (D) How many notes and layers play: thins or fills pattern steps by importance and opens/closes part and drum-lane Density gates. Stripped back to core anchors; gated parts rest Full patterns; extra percussion and gated leads enter
Complexity (C) How intricate each beat is: busier pattern tiers, bigger melodic leaps, intricate Loop answers, ratchets and flams, interplay strength, chord extensions (7th/9th/sus4/6th), and chord wandering. Never moves sound controls. Plain patterns, tight intervals, simpler chords, rare ornaments Syncopated tiers, wider leaps, rich chords, frequent ratchets/flams
Motion (M) How much and how fast things evolve: line mutation, pattern re-picks, sound drift speed/depth, fills, answers, drop-outs, and how quickly Grooves move to Breaks and Builds. At 0: Arc stays put, no mutation, no fills, no drop-outs, drift pauses Fast-moving Arc, frequent fills/drop-outs, lively mutation and drift

Your macro values are the centre, not a hard ceiling. The Arc automatically pushes effective Energy and Density up during Builds and Drops and dips them during Breaks—then returns to your centre. Complexity and Motion are never moved by the Arc.

Part Bias (Per-Part Macro Offsets)

Want busy drums over a sparse bass, or a bright lead over a dark pad? Each part has a signed Part bias (−50 to +50) on every macro. It shifts that part above or below the global macro value while easing smoothly near 0 and 50, so the biased part still rides Breaks, Builds, and Drops with the rest of the track.

  • Set Part bias: Focus a part (hold its row on SONG, double-tap to lock, or lock with AFFECT ENTIRE on CLIP), then turn the F8 gold knobs or tap/hold the macro faders on the KEYBOARD page.
  • Reset Part bias: Choose RESET PART BIAS in the Perform menu (resets the focused part, or all parts if nothing is focused).
  • Kept on Regenerate: Regenerate keeps your Part biases. A genre change resets all biases to 0.

6. The Arc & Its Sections

The Arc gives your music song structure automatically. It cycles through five Sections around your Recipe's Energy and Density values:

  • Intro: Plays when you press PLAY from stop (1–2 phrases). Density rises from the lowest part gate up to your centre, bringing parts in gradually. Press SHIFT+PLAY to skip it.
  • Groove: Home. Macros sit at your Recipe values. Automatic fills and drop-outs happen here. Higher Motion shortens how many phrases a Groove lasts before moving on; at Motion 0, the Groove plays indefinitely.
  • Break: Strips back to each genre's kept parts (for example, pads and hats) and dips Energy and Density while filters close and reverb washes in.
  • Build: Ramps Energy and Density up above your centre, opens filters/high-pass (Rise), triggers the Riser FX, and plays a climbing snare roll. A Build shorter than one phrase tucks into the end of the phrase before it so the Drop always lands on a phrase line.
  • Gap: Not a Section of its own—an optional brief silence (none, 1 beat, or 1 bar depending on genre) right before the Drop.
  • Drop: The moment a Build lands back in Groove. Every allowed part enters, an Impact and Crash hit, gesture filters snap open (Reset), and Energy/Density get a temporary lift that decays back to centre.
  • Outro: Queued by holding the End sidebar pad for 0.6 s. Energy and Density fall as parts exit, and transport stops cleanly at the end of the Outro.

Headroom: If you turn Energy or Density near 50 (or near 0), the Arc automatically scales its lifts (or dips) to fit the remaining headroom so curves stay smooth instead of slamming flat.

7. Perform: Playing Live

Perform runs in its own live song—you cannot jump into stock Clip or Arranger views while Perform is active. Everything you need sits on three grid pages, the permanent sidebar, the OLED, and the panel controls.

Panel shorthand used below: X = horizontal scroll encoder (◄►), Y = vertical scroll encoder (▲▼), F1–F8 = the 8 mod buttons beside the two gold knobs (top to bottom).

Pad Grids & Permanent Sidebar

Switch grid pages with SONG, CLIP, and KEYBOARD (their LEDs show which page is open). The two rightmost columns—MUTE/LAUNCH and AUDITION/SECTION—stay the same on every page.

SONG Page (Arc Map) & Sidebar

Sidebar rules (same on every grid page):

  • MUTE/LAUNCH (rows 1–6): Tap to mute/unmute a part at the next bar line (hold SHIFT for immediate mute at the next step). Hold X + tap a MUTE pad to solo. Green = active, red = muted, blue = solo, dim = silenced by the Arc (gate closed, Break, drop-out, or soloed out).
  • AUDITION/SECTION (rows 1–3): Force Groove (row 1, green), Break (row 2, blue), or Build (row 3, orange). Starts at the next phrase line (a short Build starts early enough to end on a phrase line). Tap again to cancel while queued.
  • AUDITION/SECTION row 5: Hold the current Section (white while held). A Build always finishes to its Drop and then holds the Groove.
  • AUDITION/SECTION row 8: End (purple). Hold for 0.6 s to queue the Outro; tap to cancel while queued. Disabled while a menu, browser, or sound editor is open on top.
  • While stopped, sidebar pads do nothing—mutes, solos, forces, and holds are live performance state cleared on stop.

CLIP Page (One Part in Depth)

KEYBOARD Page (Performance Pads & Stock Performance View)

Stock Performance View (Song FX): Press KEYBOARD a second time while already on the KEYBOARD page (the KEYBOARD LED blinks) to flip to the stock Deluge Performance View song FX pads (LPF, HPF, reverb, delay, mod FX, decimation, bitcrush, stutter). Perform's sidebar, gold knobs, OLED, and menus stay live around it. Press KEYBOARD again to return.

OLED Screens & Peeks

The top status line is always visible, with a thin phrase-progress bar underneath:

  • Left: Current Section and phrase count (GROOVE 2, BREAK 1/2), STOPPED, or—on the SONG page when a part is focused—that part's name. Adds HELD (or H) when the Arc is held.
  • Right: Inverted tag for the next queued action with its countdown (e.g. >BR3 = Break in 3 bars, + if more are queued), or bar.beat when nothing is queued. Shows EXT under an external clock, and a Capture dot at the far right (once a piece has played; filled = marked, hollow = unmarked, blinking = log full).

The main area follows your current grid page:

ARC page (on SONG): Now (inverted) + next 7 phrases. Queued boxes (BR) blink inverted; a Drop box has a vertical bar inside its right edge.
PART page (on CLIP): Part + preset name, take & freeze star (T2/3*), and the 6 controls row-by-row (BRI|THI, DEC|SPA, DRI|WOB; bar = home, tick = live; unused controls show label only).
MACROS page (on KEYBOARD): Four macro bars (fill = Recipe centre, tick = where the Arc has it right now).

Peeks (turn SELECT): Turning SELECT temporarily shows one of three info views for 4 seconds—first turn right shows QUEUE (all queued actions with countdowns), first turn left shows RECIPE (genre, tempo & swing, key/scale); keep turning to cycle RECIPE ↔ QUEUE ↔ CAPTURE (piece mark status, marked count, and log status: ON CARD, FULL, or CARD ERROR). Macro values show on the SONG page while stopped and on the MACROS page.

Buttons, Gold Knobs & Focus

How Focus Works

Focus chooses whether the gold knobs and part buttons act on the whole Recipe or on one specific part:

  • Nothing focused (Recipe level): The AFFECT ENTIRE LED is lit. F8 turns global macros; F1–F7 stay stock song FX.
  • Temporary focus: Hold a part's row on the SONG page. Releases when you lift your finger.
  • Locked focus: Double-tap a part's row on SONG (within 0.35 s), or press AFFECT ENTIRE on CLIP to lock/unlock the shown part. Double-tap again or press AFFECT ENTIRE on SONG/KEYBOARD to unlock.
  • Focus is dropped automatically when you stop playback, leave Perform, or switch to a new roster (genre change, Regenerate, or Load). A Restart (AS IT IS or AS IT BEGAN) keeps Focus.

Regenerate, Genre Changes & Transitions

When you pick REGENERATE, another genre in GENRE ▸, or load a Recipe file mid-set, Perform opens the Regenerate page (titled REGENERATE, <GENRE>?, or <FILENAME>?):

  • TRANSITION: Pick how the handover plays—DROP, FADE, or CUT. Defaults to the incoming genre's preferred entry (FADE for Ambient, DROP for all others).
  • VARIANTS ▸: Narrow any variant set of the incoming genre before rolling (always opens on RANDOM; omitted on a Recipe Load because the file brings its own variants).
  • GO: The cursor starts on GO, so SELECT, SELECT confirms immediately.
Transition Style How It Sounds Tempo & Swing
DROP
(Build into a Drop)		 Incoming sounds preload silently, then the playing genre plays a Build and the new piece lands on a Drop. Tempo glides across the Build. Swing switches cleanly on the Drop.
FADE
(Fade through)		 Outgoing genre plays its Outro (with Close & Wash), reaches the seam, and the new piece enters with its own Intro. Tempo glides across the Outro. Swing switches at the seam.
CUT
(Hard cut at phrase)		 No Build and no Outro. The new piece starts in Groove on the next phrase line after preloading finishes. Tempo glides over your GLIDE setting (or jumps if Instant). Swing switches on the cut.

Smart Key Lean: On a genre change or Regenerate, the key roll leans strongly (65%) toward keys sharing 6 or 7 notes with the key already playing, so transitions stay musical. Macros always carry over; outgoing parts get an echo/reverb Throw on their final hit before releasing.

Cancelling: Tap BACK (or pick CANCEL CHANGE in the menu) any time while the incoming sounds are still loading or waiting for the Build/Outro/cut line. Once the Build or Outro begins, the transition is committed.

Variant Sets & Drop Changes

All eight built-in genres include variant sets: musical rule variations drawn per piece alongside tempo and key.

  • Narrowing before a roll: On the Regenerate / genre-change page (and in Compose), open VARIANTS ▸ and tick the variants you want to allow. Every tick on shows RANDOM; one tick locks that variant (e.g. STEPPERS); several ticks show 2 OF 3. You cannot untick the last remaining choice.
  • Live switching in Perform: Open VARIANTS ▸ in the Perform menu during a piece.
    • Live sets open the same multi-select checklist. Changing ticks queues a VARIANT DROP (shown as VAR in the status line) that lands at the next Drop. If the currently playing variant is still ticked, it stays; otherwise a new one is drawn from your ticks. While stopped, changes apply immediately.
    • Non-live sets (sets that change song structure or a part's pattern pool, such as House's BASS or Psytrance's LEAD) display AT REGEN when pushed—change them on the Regenerate page instead.
  • Automatic Drop changes: Roots Dub's DRUMS set can switch on its own between ONE DROP, ROCKERS, and STEPPERS on Drops (40% chance per Drop) within whatever variants you allow. Tick just one drum style if you want to lock it for the rest of the piece.

Freeze, Fills, Drop-outs & Punch-ins

  • Freeze (CROSS-SCREEN or CLIP Pad 1): Locks the focused part so it repeats its current phrase and stops mutating. Macros, mutes, Break rules, and sound Drift still act on top. Unfreezing jumps seamlessly back to the live timeline.
  • Fills: The Generator automatically plays fills at phrase ends (more often at higher Motion). Hold SYNC-SCALING or any of the 12 FILL pads on KEYBOARD to play the genre's signature held fill immediately (hold SHIFT too for a fresh random fill pick). Across a phrase line, a held fill pauses for the first step (one 16th) so phrase downbeats stay clear, then carries on from the next step. Ambient has no fills.
  • Drop-outs: Built into Roots Dub, Dub Techno, House, Classic Psytrance, and Ambient. During a Groove, specific parts or drum lanes drop silent for 1–16 bars—often throwing their last hit into reverb and delay—while notes already sounding ring out cleanly. Higher Motion makes drop-outs happen more often.
  • Punch-ins (KEYBOARD page): Immediate performance effects on columns 9–10 (Drums lane mutes) and columns 15–16 (HAT ROLL 32nds, SNARE ROLL 16ths, FLAM on backbeats, DOUBLE KICK, and PUMP sidechain ducking under the kick). All follow the 0.3 s rule: tap (< 0.3 s) to latch on, hold (≥ 0.3 s) for momentary effect that springs back on release. Pressing and releasing a latched pad turns it off regardless of how long you hold it, and latched punch-ins stay on across a stop (unlike mutes, solos, and freezes). Note that FLAM is often silent in Techno at default macros because Techno has no clap and only sparse rim/snare hits.

Sound Controls, Homes & Choosing Sounds

The Generator never automates volume or pan—your mix stays yours. Instead, it shapes sounds through six sound controls: Brightness, Thin (high-pass), Decay, Space (reverb & throw delay), Drive, and Wobble (mod FX / LFOs). Each control only moves parameters that the loaded preset actually uses (for example, Brightness moves the low-pass cutoff on subtractive synths, or modulator levels on FM synths).

Every control adds three automatic layers around a sound's home (its saved preset value, or wherever you turn it):

  1. Macro layer: Effective Energy makes sounds brighter, shorter, more driven, and drier as it rises.
  2. Gestures: Arc-locked moves—Close (darkens into Breaks/Outros), Wash (adds Space in Breaks/Outros), Rise (sweeps Thin and Brightness up across Builds/Intros), Throw (spikes reverb and synced delay on a part's last hit before a Break, Gap, drop-out, or transition), and Reset (snaps all gestures back to 0 on Drops).
  3. Drift & Sweeps: Slow, smooth wandering on Brightness, Decay, Space, and Wobble—or a phrase-locked filter Sweep on acid lines, pads, and psy leads.
Turning Knobs Never Fights the Generator

Whenever you turn a gold knob, tap a CLIP fader, edit in the Sound Editor, or send MIDI CC on a sound-controlled parameter, you move its home. The Generator keeps breathing smoothly around your new position instead of snapping back.

Swapping Sounds Live

  • Re-roll from palette: Tap NEW SOUND on CLIP (or press SYNTH on Drums/FX, KIT on a synth). The new sound preloads silently and swaps in on the next phrase line. SHIFT + that button returns to the previous sound.
  • Pick any preset (SYNTH on a focused synth part): Opens the preset browser across your whole SD card (not just the palette). Scroll freely while music plays; press SELECT to preload and swap at the next phrase line.
  • Pick any drum or FX sample (KIT on a focused Drums/FX part): Opens the lane list—RE-ROLL ALL at the top, followed by every lane (KICK, SNARE, etc.). Pick a lane to browse audio files on the card (or SYNTH PRESET on FX lanes that accept synths).

MIDI Control & External Clock

  • Macro CCs: Send CC 114 (Energy), CC 115 (Density), CC 116 (Complexity), and CC 117 (Motion) on your stock MIDI-follow channels (A, B, or C). Values 0–127 map to 0–50, move the Recipe centre, and send MIDI feedback.
  • MIDI-follow & Learn: MIDI-follow CCs on channels A–C target whatever the gold knobs point at (the focused part or Song FX). Per-track channels (Tracks 1–16) are disabled in Perform. Learned CCs work on the current Output until it is replaced.
  • Global MIDI commands: PLAY, FILL, SHIFT, RECORD (Capture mark), TAP, and TRANSPOSE (queues a key change at the next phrase line) all work. External MIDI notes, pitch bend, mod wheel, and MPE are dropped.
  • External clock: When synced to MIDI/DIN/CV clock, the status line shows EXT. The master clock owns tempo (no tempo glides, TAP ignored, TEMPO turn shows EXT), while SHIFT+TEMPO still glides swing and X+TEMPO nudges sync. Entering Perform stops playback and waits for the master's Start or Continue.

8. The Compose Screen

Compose lets you set up a Recipe and render it directly into a standard, editable Deluge song. Because Compose builds a fixed song, there is no live audition on this screen—to hear and jam with a Recipe first, use Perform (both modes share the same Current Recipe).

The OLED title shows the current genre, tempo, key, and scale (scrolling if wide). Press SAVE at any time to save the Recipe, or LOAD to load another.

Building & What You Get

While rendering, the OLED shows COMPOSING with a progress bar and BACK = CANCEL. Your old song keeps playing while it builds unless RAM is tight, in which case playback stops first. When done, the new song swaps in stopped in Arranger view, unsaved, and pre-named GEN <GENRE> <seed> (for example GEN ACID TECHNO 1A2B3C4).

  • Length: Compose never chops a Break or Build in half—once it passes your target length, it finishes the Groove cleanly and adds the genre's Outro.
  • Session & Arranger views: Every sounding part gets one full-song clip placed in Arranger view and grouped into one session section in Session view. Parts that stayed silent throughout get no clip.
  • Editable notes & automation: Notes are written at 100% probability with exact tick nudges for swing/Lean/ornaments. Sound controls become thinned, editable automation curves on real synth parameters (never volume or pan).
  • Plain Deluge song: Once built, it is a completely normal Deluge song—save it with SAVE, add clips, chop parts, or tweak synths as usual.

9. Capture: Keeping Live Pieces

Capture turns your live Perform jams into editable Deluge songs. Instead of recording raw audio, Perform quietly logs every move you make—macro turns, Part biases, mutes, solos, fills, punch-ins, key/tempo changes, sound swaps, and knob edits—to GENERATIVE/CAPTURE/ on your SD card.

Marking Pieces While You Play

  • A piece runs from PLAY, a Restart, a Regenerate, a genre change, or a mid-set Load until you stop or start the next piece.
  • Tap RECORD (playing or stopped) to mark or unmark the current piece. The RECORD LED lights up and the status-line Capture dot fills in. Marking includes everything played before and after you pressed RECORD for that whole piece.
  • Open CAPTURE ▸ in the Perform menu to see your marked pieces plus the current (or last) piece (up to 16 newest listed; up to 32 kept). Push to toggle a piece's mark. Unmarked pieces leave the table as soon as the next piece begins, so mark a piece before starting the next one if you want to keep it.
  • Forgot to press RECORD? No problem—the Leave menu always offers to keep your last piece even if you never marked it.

Building Songs When You Leave

Hold BACK to open the Leave menu and choose what to keep: MARKED + LAST, MARKED ONLY, MARKED (when the last piece is already marked), LAST (when nothing was marked), NOTHING / LEAVE (blank session song), or CANCEL. Capture builds and saves each kept piece one at a time directly into SONGS/ as GEN <GENRE> <seed> LIVE (adding B, C, … if that name already exists—it never overwrites). You land in Arranger view on the last saved song.

During the handover, the OLED shows BUILDING n OF m and SAVING n OF m with a progress bar and BACK = STOP. Press BACK to stop early (songs already saved remain safely on the card; any save already writing finishes first). If a card write fails mid-Capture, Capture stops and shows the stock card error plus n OF m SAVED, leaving the unsaved song open so you can still try SAVE. If power is lost during a set, any pieces you had already marked are recovered automatically the next time you enter Perform (up to the last ~10-second flush).

What Capture Keeps—and Its Limits

  • Tempo changes: Saved as song-level tempo automation—play the song in Arranger view to hear tempo glides.
  • Swing changes: Baked directly into note tick positions.
  • Key & scale changes: Automatically split pitched clips at the change so earlier clips stay in scale mode.
  • Sound swaps & mid-piece synth setting changes: Start a fresh track from the swap step so both the old and new sounds play cleanly.
  • Very long pieces: To keep saved songs reliably loadable, any piece that exceeds ~25–30 minutes automatically splits at a clean Section boundary into sequential songs: … LIVE, … LIVE 2, … LIVE 3.
  • Not captured in v1: Stock Stutter and the PUMP punch-in cannot be represented as normal song automation in v1, so captured songs play without those two effects.

10. Recipe Files: Saving & Loading

Recipe files are tiny XML files stored in RECIPES/ at the root of your SD card (created automatically on first save). They keep your Recipes separate from your normal songs.

Saved in a Recipe File

Genre & version, tempo, swing, key, scale, the 4 macros, octave shifts, Part biases, all sound file paths, variant picks & allowed masks, seed, and every part's take.

Not Saved

Mutes, solos, freezes, knob/sound-control homes, Arc position, Compose length, Glide setting, and live steering history (use Capture for replaying a live performance).

Saving (SAVE)

  • Naming: A Recipe that has never been saved or loaded starts with the default name <GENRE> <seed> (for example ACID TECHNO 1A2B3C4). After any save or load, the next save suggests the next free letter suffix appended directly to the remembered name with no space (NIGHT DRIVE → NIGHT DRIVEA, or ACID TECHNO 1A2B3C4A).
  • Quick save (playing in Perform): Pressing SAVE saves immediately under the suggested name without stopping playback or changing the grid. Quick save never overwrites (if no letter suffix is free, it shows NO FREE NAME).
  • Full save UI (stopped, in Compose, or SHIFT+SAVE): Opens the QWERTY pad keyboard and RECIPES/ browser so you can type a custom name. If no letter suffix is free, it offers the remembered (or default) name and asks OVERWRITE? before replacing it.

Loading (LOAD) & Sharing

  • Press LOAD in Perform or Compose (or pick FILE... from the entry menus) to browse RECIPES/.
  • In Perform while playing: Choosing a file opens the Regenerate page (TRANSITION + GO) so the file transitions in smoothly at a Drop, Fade, or Cut.
  • In Perform while stopped: No transition is asked; the file's sounds preload directly under the working animation (press BACK to cancel).
  • Missing sounds: If a file references a preset or sample missing from your SD card, Generative mode re-rolls just that missing sound from your card's palette and shows 1 SOUND REPLACED or N SOUNDS REPLACED. Your notes stay the same.
  • Genre version notices: If a firmware update retuned a genre since the file was saved, the file still loads and briefly pops up OLDER <GENRE> (or NEWER <GENRE>). Re-saving updates the file to the current version.

11. The Generative Pack

The firmware contains no embedded audio or synth patches. While Generative mode uses your factory Deluge card content wherever possible, the Generative pack adds essential custom presets and samples: risers, drop impacts, the dub siren, dry dub-techno chord presets, ambient drones/basses/brushes/mallets, a tight psy rolling bass, and tuned drum swaps.

Installing on Your SD Card

  1. Download the Generative pack .zip attached to the firmware release.
  2. Unzip it straight onto the root of your SD card (merge into existing SYNTHS/ and SAMPLES/ folders—do not replace the factory files inside them).
  3. Verify that your SD card has this structure:
SD CARD ROOT/
├── SYNTHS/
│   └── GENERATIVE/          ← Custom synth presets + FX/ presets
├── SAMPLES/
│   ├── FX/Riser/            ← Pack risers (+ Riser/Short/ for Roots Dub)
│   └── DRUMS/               ← GEN-prefixed one-shots in factory drum folders
├── GENERATIVE/
│   ├── LICENSES.md          ← Pack origins & licences
│   ├── PALETTES/            ← (Optional) your custom palette override XMLs
│   └── CAPTURE/             ← Created by firmware for live Capture logs
└── RECIPES/                 ← Created by firmware when saving Recipes
Running Without the Pack?

Generative mode will still start: missing individual picks quietly fall back to the next entry in the genre's palette. A SOUND MISSING warning appears only if the entire fallback chain fails (leaving a synth part on the default synth patch, or a drum/FX lane silent). However, Risers, Impacts, Roots Dub's Siren, and Ambient's custom percussion and basses need the pack to sound as designed.

Optional: Custom Palette Overrides

Advanced users can swap which presets or sample folders a genre draws from by placing an XML file at GENERATIVE/PALETTES/<GENRE NAME>.XML—using the genre's exact OLED name (for example, ACID TECHNO.XML, HOUSE.XML, ROOTS DUB.XML, CLASSIC PSY.XML, or AMBIENT.XML). Any part, role, or drum lane you list overrides only that entry; everything else keeps the built-in palette. Keep override files under 8 KB and within the format's preset, tag, and folder limits—a file that exceeds any limit or is malformed is ignored completely and the built-in palette plays instead.

12. Practical Tips

  • Leave headroom around 25: Keeping Energy and Density near the middle lets Breaks dip noticeably and Drops hit with full impact. If you peg Density at 50, Drops have nowhere left to lift.
  • Hold a Section vs. Motion 0: Tap the Hold sidebar pad (row 5) to stay in the current Section while letting mutation, fills, drop-outs, and sound drift keep moving. Turn Motion to 0 when you want to freeze everything—stopping the Arc from advancing and turning off mutation, fills, drop-outs, and drift.
  • Quick-save happy accidents: Tap SAVE while playing to snapshot your Recipe without interrupting the beat (see Recipe Files).
  • Restart clean vs. Restart original: Press X+PLAY (AS IT IS) to restart from bar 1 keeping your current knob tweaks and takes. Use RESTART ▸ AS IT BEGAN when you steered too far off course and want to snap back to how the piece started.
  • Big tempo jumps? Use FADE: Leaving Roots Dub (75 BPM) for Psytrance (142 BPM) on DROP slams the tempo across a single 1-bar Build. Pick FADE for a smoother glide across the Outro.
  • Sculpt one part fast: Double-tap a part on SONG to lock focus, jump to KEYBOARD to ride its Part bias faders (hold ≥ 0.3 s for temporary swells!), or hit F7 twice for dedicated Thin / Drive knobs.
  • Jump straight into the beat: Press SHIFT+PLAY from stop whenever you want to skip the Intro and land right in a Groove.
  • Bring in Ambient's percussion: Raise Density from 29 to 47 to fade Ambient's six soft percussion lanes into Grooves one by one (see Ambient in Section 3).

Common Questions

  • A part is lit green on MUTE, or dim, and I can't hear it: A dim MUTE pad means the Arc has silenced it (its Density gate is closed, a Break excluded it, a drop-out is active, or another part is soloed). Raise Density, wait for the Drop, or check for a blue solo pad.
  • My MIDI keyboard isn't playing notes in Perform: By design in v1, Perform ignores external MIDI notes so Capture stays exact. Use MIDI-follow CCs (114–117 for macros) and global MIDI commands instead.
  • My Capture song doesn't play my tempo changes: Tempo automation lives at song level—switch to Arranger view to play it back.
  • I held End (row 8) and nothing happened: End requires a 0.6 s hold while Perform is the top screen. Close any open menu, browser, or sound editor first.
  • A fader row on CLIP is completely dark: Either no part has been focused yet (focus one on SONG first), the part is FX (whose controls live per lane), or the loaded preset doesn't use any parameter for that control.

14. Glossary

Arc
The Generator's automatic progression through Sections (Intro, Groove, Break, Build, Outro) around your Recipe's Energy and Density values.
Capture
Replaying live Perform pieces from their steering log into editable Deluge songs saved in SONGS/ when you leave Perform.
Compose
The mode that edits a Recipe and renders it into a normal, editable Deluge song in Session and Arranger views.
Current Recipe
The active Recipe (with its seed and takes) kept in RAM until power-off and shared between Perform and Compose.
Drop
The moment a Build lands back in Groove, bringing all allowed parts back in with a temporary Energy and Density lift and snapping gesture filters open.
Drop-out
Whole parts or drum lanes dropping silent for 1–16 bars during a Groove (often with their last hit thrown into reverb and delay) while sounding notes ring out.
Focus
The part currently targeted by the gold knobs, CLIP/part buttons, and KEYBOARD bias faders—held on SONG, or locked by double-tap or AFFECT ENTIRE on CLIP.
Gap
An optional brief silence (1 beat or 1 bar) at the very end of a Build, right before the Drop.
Generative Pack
The companion SD card zip of custom presets and samples (risers, impacts, siren, dry dub-techno stabs, ambient drones/basses/percussion, psy bass) that complements factory content.
Home
The baseline position of a sound-controlled parameter (its preset value, or wherever you turn it). Macros, gestures, and drift move around home without fighting your edits.
Lane
One fixed drum or FX slot inside a Drums or FX part (Kick, Snare, Clap, Rim, Closed hat, Open hat, Ride, Crash, Tom, Perc 1, Perc 2; Riser, Fall, Impact, Siren).
Macro
One of the four 0–50 controls—Energy, Density, Complexity, Motion—that steer many musical behaviours at once across all genres.
Palette
A genre's built-in weighted list of synth presets and drum/FX samples for each part and lane. Can be overridden per genre on the SD card.
Part
One instrument voice played by the Generator (Drums, Bass, Chords, Pad, Lead, or FX; 4–6 per genre).
Part Bias
A signed offset (−50 to +50) shifting one part above or below a global macro while still letting it follow the Arc.
Perform
The live, real-time mode where the Generator plays continuously and responds immediately to your steering.
Piece
One continuous run in Perform—from Start, Restart, Regenerate, genre change, or mid-set Load until stop or the next change. Capture saves each kept piece as its own song.
Punch-in
A momentary or latched live effect on the KEYBOARD page: drum lane mutes, Hat Roll, Snare Roll, Flam, Double Kick, or Pump.
Recipe
The set of parameters (genre, musical facts, macros, octave shifts, Part biases, sound picks, and variant picks) that tells the Generator what to play. Contains no notes.
Regenerate
Starting a fresh piece in the same genre with newly rolled tempo, key, scale, sounds, and seed while keeping your macros and Part biases.
Section
One stretch of the Arc: Intro, Groove, Break, Build, or Outro. Distinct from the Deluge's own Session-view clip groups ("session sections").
Seed
A number beside the Recipe (shown as a 7-character code like 1A2B3C4) that makes every random musical decision repeatable.
Sound Control
One of six macro/gesture/drift channels that shape sounds live: Brightness, Thin, Decay, Space, Drive, and Wobble.
Take
A part's pattern re-roll counter (1–16). Stepping takes changes that part's patterns and melodies without touching any other part.
Throw
A gesture that spikes a part's reverb and synced delay feedback on its last hit before silence (Break, Gap, drop-out, or transition), letting that hit echo cleanly.
Transition
How Perform moves from one piece to the next on a genre change, Regenerate, or mid-set Load: DROP (Build into Drop), FADE (Outro into Intro), or CUT (hard cut at phrase line).
Variant Set / Variant
A genre rule with 2–8 weighted styles (such as Roots Dub's ONE DROP, ROCKERS, and STEPPERS drums). Drawn per piece, narrowable before Regenerate, and switchable at Drops for live sets.
