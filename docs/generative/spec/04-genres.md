# 4. Genres

Part of the [Generative mode spec](../spec.md). Section numbers are shared across all files.

### 4.1 What a genre is

A **genre** is a built-in, read-only style definition. It gives a new Recipe its starting values and sets the rules the Generator plays by. A Recipe refers to it by name and never copies its tables. The player can't edit a genre.

- Genres are constant tables in the Generator core, shared by firmware and harness. A new genre needs a firmware rebuild.
- Genres are **pure data** read by one generic Generator. New behaviour becomes a shared mechanism with a flag (for example "the Build rolls on snare or perc"), never code for one genre.
- Every field has a Generator-wide default, and a genre lists only the fields it changes. A genre may be written as overrides of another genre, as an authoring shortcut. Slots are optional: a genre with no drums leaves that slot empty and isn't a special case.
- Adding a field means adding it once with a default that keeps today's behaviour. It never means editing existing genres.
- A genre has **no pieces** to pick from. Variety comes from rolled facts, sound picks and the seed.
- **Subgenres.** A genre may name a **parent genre**, a table that is never played itself. The subgenre is written as overrides of its parent and inherits every field it doesn't list. In every other way a subgenre is a genre: it has its own name, version, roster, Harmony slot and palette, Regenerate stays inside it, and moving to a sibling is a genre change ([§9.7](09-arc.md#97-transitions)). The parent also groups its subgenres in the GENRE menu ([§14.1](14-entry-and-compose-screen.md#141-the-generative-menu)). A parent has no parent of its own.
- A part offers no alternatives inside a genre. Another kind of bassline, or another lead, is another subgenre.

### 4.3 Meter

Every v1 genre is 4/4 on a 16th-note grid. Phrase length is a genre value (Psytrance uses 16 bars). Triplet and shuffle feel come from swing and Lean ([§8.5](08-rhythm-and-expression.md#85-lean)).

### 4.4 Names and versions

- A genre has a stable lowercase name (such as `house`), used in Recipe files. Names are never reused.
- A subgenre's stable name is its parent's, a hyphen and its own (`techno-acid`). Its OLED name is the full one (`ACID TECHNO`), and a short name (`ACID`) labels its row under the parent in the GENRE menu.
- An OLED name is at most 14 characters, so a genre change's confirm title (`MELODIC TECHNO?`) fits its line ([§13.6](13-perform.md#136-perform-menu)). A genre data spec checks every genre.
- A parent's table carries no version. An edit to it that changes a subgenre's event stream bumps that subgenre's version.
- A genre has a **version number** that goes up whenever a retune changes its event stream. Recipe files store it. Old genre tables are not kept ([§16.2](16-recipe-files.md#162-genre-versions)). Golden files change in the same commit ([§21.2](21-testing.md#212-harness)).

### 4.5 v1 genres

The briefs below are what implementation fills tables against. Tables are hand-written, with PERF-1's genre tables as the main reference, but every value is our own, re-derived and tuned by ear. Copying PERF-1's tables row for row counts as porting (ADR 0001).

- **Techno** is a parent genre with four subgenres. The parent holds what they share: straight time, a four-on-the-floor kick, offbeat hats and a rim, and no claps on 2 and 4 (that's House). Long Builds and a kick drop at phrase ends. Groove 4–8 phrases (*tuning*). Fills: a Rest on Kick + Bass (1–4 beats) as the main entry and the held-fill entry, plus a Swap on Snare and Tom. Crash accent on Drops only (*tuning*). An FX part with Riser, Fall and Impact. Each subgenre sets its tempo range, scales, pitched parts, Harmony slot, Break keeps and palette, and may override anything else, such as its drums or its Break dip. Their pitched parts share some habits (*tuning*):
  - Minor chords over major ones. The Chord graphs favour i, iv, v, ♭VI and ♭VII, and an Extension is a 7th or a 9th far more often than a 6th.
  - Slow harmony: a chord lasts 2 bars at the least.
  - Every Pad opens over the phrase with an 8-bar Brightness Sweep ([§11.5](11-sound-controls.md#115-drift)).
  - A part that would crowd a default Groove gets a Density gate above the genre's default Density, so the Arc brings it in on Drops and the player brings it in by raising Density. The MUTE pads take any part in and out by hand ([§13.5](13-perform.md#135-control-layout)).

  The subgenres:
  - **Acid techno** (`techno-acid`, ACID TECHNO). 125–135 BPM, Minor or Phrygian. A mutating acid Loop bass with an 8-bar Sweep on its filter, and a **drone**: a pedal Sustain Pad (root and fifth) under everything in level, not in pitch (it sits above the acid line), which the Break keeps. An empty Harmony slot, so one chord throughout. Parts: Drums, Acid, Drone, FX. It is the default genre, the first row the GENRE menu opens on before any Recipe exists.
    - **Tables.** The parent table holds the Drums and FX parts, the fills, the Arc and its section rules, the gestures, and a palette of drum and FX lanes only; Acid techno's table adds its tempo range, scales, the Acid and Drone parts, Break keeps (the Drone, over the parent's Kick, Rim and Closed hat; Builds keep the same) and its palette. The parent's Break keeps are Kick, Rim and Closed hat, so a subgenre that names none breaks down to those rather than playing a full Groove. The parent's Rest fill drops part 1, so every Techno subgenre lists its bass second. The Drone is a pedal Sustain Pad in the Pad Register's default (anchor 60, −12 to +12): the root on the tonic nearest middle C, the 5th above it. With no Chord graph nothing re-strikes it but a cut (a Section line, a key change, a new sound); it has no retrigger period and no Density gate, so it plays from the Intro, where Density brings its 5th in after the root. Its Break dips Density by 5 points, not the parent's 20, as Melodic and Detroit techno's do: with the parent's dip the Drone played its bare root and the drums thinned to a few hits, so at the default Density the Drone keeps its 5th there and more drums play (*tuning*, #120). Its palette is 005 Sweet Mono Bass alone for the Acid part (165 Acid Arp, 015 Resonant Filter Bass and 097 Saturated Filter failed their ear check, #120) and the drone pads (148 Warm 5th Pad, 126 Dark FM Pad, 139 Detuned Saw Pad, 149 Cold 5th Pad).
  - **Melodic techno** (`techno-melodic`, MELODIC TECHNO). 122–128 BPM, Minor, Phrygian or Harmonic Minor. A Chord graph with home i and the moves i–♭VI, ♭VI–♭VII, ♭VII–i, i–iv, iv–♭VI and i–♭VII, 2 bars per chord, cadence target ♭VII every 4 chords, and Extension ceiling 1. In Harmonic Minor the 7th degree's chord is diminished, so the guard swaps the cadence to V and its raised leading tone, which is what that scale is there for. A rolling bass Loop on the three 16ths after each beat, mostly root and octave, the 5th now and then, rare slides, in the Bass Register's default (anchor C2): the root sounds between F♯1 and F2 ([§10.6](10-sounds.md#106-load-time-settings)). Its patterns leave the kick's steps empty, so it needs no Avoid rule. For that its Drums are the parent's with one change: the kick stays four on the floor at every Complexity, without the parent's pickups off the beats. A Pad voicing each chord, and an Arp lead on 16ths, Up or Up-down over one or two octaves, with a 16-bar Sweep. Neither is gated: Density thins the Arp by step importance instead. Break keeps: Pad, Arp and Closed hat, so the breakdown is the pad and the arp alone. Its kept parts have few anchors, so its Break dips Density by 5 points instead of the parent's 20, which would thin the Break to almost nothing at the default Density (*tuning*). Palette, trimmed by ear (#120 round 1): the Bass plays 000 Rich Saw Bass and 012 Blunt Sync Bass (047 and 050 dropped), the Pad 139 Detuned Saw Pad, 151 Radiant FM Pad and 142 Phaser (153 dropped), the Arp 058 FM Rising Attack and 067 Marimba (167 dropped). Parts: Drums, Bass, Pad, Arp, FX.
  - **Detroit techno** (`techno-detroit`, DETROIT TECHNO). 125–135 BPM, Minor or Dorian. A Chord graph that matters: home i, the moves i–♭VII, i–iv, i–v, iv–v and ♭VI–♭VII, with Dorian's i–IV from the colour moves, 2 bars per chord, repeat weight 0. The walk's phrase rule keeps home off a phrase's last chord, so bars 7–8 always turn the loop round. Extension floor 1 and ceiling 2, so every chord carries an Extension, usually the 7th, and a second comes with Complexity. A Bass Loop on root rhythm: the chord root on most beats and on the & before the next, the octave and 5th with Complexity. A Strings Pad, a Stab (Chords) on syncopated 16ths that keep off the 1, and a Motif lead: a one- or two-bar riff stored as chord positions, so it follows each change. The Motif is gated at 27 (*tuning*), so it plays the whole Drop and not the Groove. Break keeps: Strings, Stab and Closed hat. Parts: Drums, Bass, Strings, Stab, Motif, FX.
    - **Tables.** The parent's Drums and FX. Its graph plays each of the brief's moves both ways, the way back to i by the home pull (i–♭VII 30, i–iv 25, i–v 15, iv–v 20, v–iv 10, ♭VI–♭VII 30, ♭VII–♭VI 20, home pull 20, in the colour moves' units; no cadence target): read one way only, ♭VI is never reached and a phrase's last chord on v or ♭VII has only home to go to, which the phrase rule excludes, so the walk would repeat it. In Dorian ♭VI is the diminished vi, which the guard thins. The Bass (`BASS`) plays the root on the beats and the &s, the 1 anchored, a 16th pickup on the a from tier 1, the 5th from Complexity 34 (a leap of 0 to 6) and the octave above at up to 250 per-mille; its one Answer is the octave, so the 5th never plays at Complexity 0. Its Register is the Bass default (anchor C2): the root sounds between F♯1 and F2 ([§10.6](10-sounds.md#106-load-time-settings)). The Strings (`STRINGS`) are a Sustain Pad in the Pad Register's default, voicing every chord with its Extension: their first Extension is an anchor, as the root is (§5.2), so no jitter draw, whatever the seed and take, thins it out. It plays wherever the Strings do, in the Groove, the Break's dip and the Build, and in the Intro and Outro, where low Density thins the Strings to the root and the Extension, the 5th and the 3rd coming in after them. The Stab (`STAB`) is anchored on the a of 1, then &s and a's, with a 36-tick gate, in the Chords Register's default. The Motif (`MOTIF`) plays one- and two-bar riffs from its anchored 1, mostly the root, 3rd and 5th, in the Lead Register's default, gated at 27: the Drop's lift (+8) takes the default Density to 33, and with the gate's hysteresis (2) it stays open while any lift is left, so it plays the whole Drop and stops on the bar where the lift has decayed to the default Density (25). The Break dips Density by 5, not 20, as Melodic techno's does: the closed hat has no anchors. Its palette: Bass 007 Detuned Saw Bass, 050 FM Basic Bass, 012 Blunt Sync Bass (each saved two octaves down, played at 0); Strings 019 Fizzy Strings, 134 Melody String, 131 Warm Strings; Stab 088 Yelp Chords, 071 Rhodes; Motif 021 80s TV Lead, 058 FM Rising Attack (047 Basic Dirty Bass, 086 Techno Organ, 083 Dark Chorus and 069 FM Bells 2 were dropped by ear, #120; not 068 FM Bells 1, whose oscillator sits an octave up); Techno's lanes. No new pack content. Levels (*tuning*, delugemu A/B captures with one part gated out): the Strings at 60 % of their preset's volume and the Stab at 80 %, so they sit about 10 and 4.5 dB under the rest of the mix; the Motif at its preset's, about 8.5 dB under.

An empty Harmony slot with Extension floor 1 and ceiling 2: one m7 or m9 chord throughout, repeated by the stab while its sound moves (*tuning*: a slow i–iv walk at 8 bars per chord if one chord is too static). A Stab (Chords) on the & and the 16th after it, soft, with ghosted repeats, echoing from a dotted-8th delay setup with delay Throws ([§10.6](10-sounds.md#106-load-time-settings), [§11.4](11-sound-controls.md#114-gestures)), as Dub's skank does, and Dub's drop-out: the Stab out for 2 bars, thrown into its echo (#322). The Stab (`STAB`) is anchored on one & a bar, with the 16th after it ghosted, and ghosted repeats on later &s and a's that grow with Complexity, every line with a repeat of importance 8 on each bar's last & (so the drop-out's Throw has a hit to spike on, as Dub's skank lines have); it has a 16-bar Brightness Sweep, the sound moving under the one chord. Break keeps: Stab, Pad and Rim, with the parent's Density dip (−20): at the default Density the Break is the Stab's anchored & each bar, the Pad's bare root (its 5th from Density 35) and the rim on the a of 2. Its palette: Sub 002 Basic Square Bass, 050 FM Basic Bass (each saved two octaves down, played at 0; 047 Basic Dirty Bass was dropped by ear, #120); Stab `GEN Echo Chord Dry` (the main pick), `GEN Short Sharp Dry`, `GEN Kyoto Phono Dry`, the pack's copies of 037 Echo Chord, 082 Short Sharp Delay and 072 Kyoto Phono with their own echo off ([§18.2](18-generative-pack.md#182-contents)), then the three originals at weight 0, which load only through the fallback chain on a card without the pack; Pad 131 Warm Strings, 139 Detuned Saw Pad, 126 Dark FM Pad; Techno's lanes, with only `SAMPLES/DRUMS/Shaker` on Perc 2. No new pack content.
