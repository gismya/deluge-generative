# 20. Budgets and measured facts

Part of the [Generative mode spec](../spec.md). Section numbers are shared across all files.

Measured on a real OLED Deluge unless marked.

| Fact | Value |
|---|---|
| CPU | one Cortex-A9 at 400 MHz, cooperative scheduler. Ticks run inside the audio routine, which has about 2.9 ms of buffer (128 samples at 44.1 kHz) |
| Per-tick work | keep it in the tens of µs, with no card access and no allocation (estimate) |
| Task runs | keep each well under 3 ms, or split and yield. State read by the tick path must be consistent across yields |
| Voice starts | 4 per render window, 1 under heavy load. Extras wait one render: the audio routine targets 1.45 ms and is at most 2.9 ms. Above about 215 BPM a tick is shorter than a render, so the per-tick budget no longer holds. No fixed voice cap; culling is driven by CPU load |
| Fast heap free | 1002 K with no song; 749 K with a typical song; 694 K with that song plus a Perform roster playing (RelWithDebInfo, Release has about 46 K more) |
| Two songs in RAM | 591 K free at the swap with two typical songs, 273 K with two heavy ones (largest block 148 K). No late renders |
| Perform roster | about 162 K for the largest roster: 11 drum and 4 FX lanes at about 8.1 K plus 4 synths at about 10 K. Two rosters are about 323 K, which fits next to a heavy song (273 K free with two heavy songs is the closest measurement). Perform's song and the Generator blocks aren't in this figure yet |
| Steering log | one fixed 128 KB buffer in SDRAM, allocated on Perform entry ([§17.2](17-capture.md#172-the-steering-log)), not fast heap. A piece header is about 6.5 KB for Acid techno's roster and 7.5 KB for the largest (measured, H13) |
| Steering queue | 64 commands, fixed, in the Perform driver ([§2.6](02-architecture.md#26-steering-into-the-core)) |
| Kit lane | about 8.1 KB fast heap plus the sample in SDRAM. 10–15 ms per cold lane while playing; a 12-lane kit under 0.2 s |
| Synth preset load | 5.3 ms, 10 K fast heap |
| Delay Throw buffers | a delay Throw that lifts feedback from 0 makes the stock render allocate the set's delay buffer from SDRAM inside the render (`allocLowSpeed`): up to 88,200 stereo samples, about 690 KB, at a long synced note and a slow tempo, freed once the repeats run out ([§10.6](10-sounds.md#106-load-time-settings)). Every set with a delay setup can hold one at once: in the largest roster four pitched parts, the Drums set its Throw acts on and four FX lanes, nine buffers, about 6.2 MB worst case. A Groove leaves about 2.05 MB of the external heap free (delugemu, Acid techno), so from the third full buffer on the allocator takes the room from the stealable region, evicting cached sample data, inside the render; if that fails too, the delay stays silent (`INSUFFICIENT_RAM`). A genre's tuning keeps the sets that throw together, and their notes, to what fits. `perform_delay_throws.txt` throws two at once at 60 BPM (a dotted half on the Bass, a dotted quarter on the kit): in delugemu the external heap's free room went from about 2,052,700 B in the Groove to 640,000–825,000 B in the Break (1.2–1.4 MB for the two), and a phrase later was back in two runs of four; in the other two, 164–186 KB was still in use at that line (not traced; after leaving, the heap matched entry in every run). On hardware (Release test-port build at 0119faaab, `perform_delay_throws.txt` 48/0): free external heap 2,052,952 B before entry, 2,052,352 B in the Groove, 618,752 B in the Break with the two Throws' buffers, and 1,865,984 B a phrase later. The 187 KB still in use there shows up on hardware too, and comes back on leaving: 2,053,248 B after leave. `gen_h16`: no culls in all six windows, 1–3 voices, `render_max_us` 303–836 (612, 331, 735, 438, 836, 303), so the buffers' allocation inside the render made no late render. Under Dub's own playing on hardware (#131, below), its Throws took at most about 391 KB at once, freed by the next Section line. Dub techno's Stab (#322, below) throws from home 0 (the pack's dry presets), so each drop-out's Throw allocates its buffer; at its Section lines the external heap was back at its Groove level |
| Entering Perform | 129–197 ms with Acid techno's roster, every sample read from the card; 190 ms with Detroit techno's six parts while the heavy song Ttt plays (H9, below) |
| Silent incoming Kits | linked next to a playing heavy song, an 11-lane Kit costs 59 µs and a 4-row FX Kit 42 µs per 128-sample buffer: about 3.5% CPU together, with no late renders ([Measure silent incoming Kits and retiring Outputs on a Deluge][t83]). The cost is per render, so it rises when renders are short |
| Linking an incoming roster | 6–7 ms for three Outputs. The largest single link (clip, NoteRows and Output, no yield) took 1.6 ms. No late renders |
| Freeing a retired Output | `deleteClipObject` with `InstrumentRemoval::DELETE` while playing: 11-lane Kit 18–24 ms in 67 yields, FX Kit 6–10.5 ms in 21, Synth 1.7–3.6 ms in 5. At most 0.93 ms between yields, no late renders. The Output stays in the render list for about one yield per NoteRow first ([§12.9](12-runtime.md#129-instances-and-the-transition)) |
| Compose song | measured with a clip per part per Section occurrence: 1.7 MB for 4:00, 4.7 MB for 20:00, 15.7 MB for 60:00. Clip setup is 67–75% of it, about 130 K per occurrence (about 6 K per kit row, 11 K per synth clip). Notes are about 30 B each. So [§15](15-compose-output.md#15-compose-output-shape) uses one clip per part. Measured (H19, delugemu, #136): a 20:00 Psytrance song (816–832 bars, 28,420–29,943 notes, about 11,900 nodes over two rolls) takes 1.07–1.14 MB of the general heaps, about 190 K of it the Outputs, and builds in 1.6–1.7 s; Detroit techno (696–704 bars, 27,217 notes) takes 0.89–0.96 MB. The build's own work block (the Generator, the driver) isn't counted: it is freed before the swap. About 22 B per note or node. Both save (0.3–0.4 s) and load back, with the built song still current. The §15.9 estimate for them is 6.2 and 5.4 MB: an upper bound, about 5× what they take. The sample cache isn't counted: it lives in the stealable region. With the boot song, delugemu has about 0.9 MB of fast heap, 2 MB of SDRAM's general regions and 61.6 MB of the stealable region free. Freeing the current song doesn't help the fast heap: song structures are `allocMaxSpeed` and fill it first. XML parsing is 2–5× slower while audio runs |
| Saving and loading songs | a 1.0 MB file saves in 0.36 s and loads in 2.0 s. A 10.1 MB file saves in 3.4–7.55 s but fails to load on an empty Deluge with `FILE_CORRUPTED`, so Capture splits long pieces ([§17.5](17-capture.md#175-song-shape)) |
| Out of memory | once SDRAM is full, any `operator new` throws `BAD_ALLOC`, nothing catches it, and the device freezes with `TERM`. Compose and Capture stop at an SDRAM reserve, 512 K to start ([§15.9](15-compose-output.md#159-memory-and-failures)) |
| Pad redraw | 21–22 ms of UART per full redraw, shared with the OLED. Pad sends defer behind OLED updates. The SONG page in delugemu: 100 main redraws in an 8-bar Techno phrase with nothing queued (under one per 16th), 139 with queued actions blinking (about 9.4 a second; the ceiling is about 45). On hardware (#251, e25cbe583) a whole 8-bar phrase took 116–140 with Acid techno and 82–173 with Detroit techno, the most in a phrase with a genre change queued (about 12 a second) |
| Arc plan | `Generator::planArc` (16 phrases, 2,048 steps for Techno) takes, on hardware (#251, Release test-port build at e25cbe583), 2.12–2.30 ms with Acid techno, 2.56–2.75 ms over the Detroit techno session (six parts), 2.01–2.46 ms over a genre change through the four Techno subgenres; plans cut short (an Outro's end, a change's seam) 0.2–0.8 ms. M1's build took 1.85–2.19 ms with Techno, delugemu about 0.55 ms. The SONG page's redraws in the same session: the Pad redraw row above. It runs inside `graphicsRoutine` at each phrase line and queue change, a task the audio routine can't preempt, against the 2.9 ms output buffer: it breaks the Task runs rule above (a fix is open, #251) |
| OLED | 128×43 visible, 4 rows of 21 characters or a title plus 3 rows. Redraws at most every 10 ms. `OLED::setupSideScroller` doesn't scroll while playing; the Compose title scrolls the way the stock session view scrolls a long song name |
| Tempo | the hardware plays exactly the set BPM (130.0000 by the sample clock). delugemu runs about 0.5% slow |
| Stability | the POC's hook ran 31 minutes with no leak, drift, stuck notes or late renders, and followed swing and tempo changes exactly |
| `new` | global `operator new` and std containers use only a 2 MiB SDRAM pool and throw when full. The Generator doesn't use them ([§2.1](02-architecture.md#21-the-generator-core)) |
| Song loads | the "buttons and pads" task can run up to 20 ms without yielding during a song load |
| System clock | `getSystemTime()` reads about 0.8% short against real time |

The core is 13,532 B of state per Generator block on ARM (`sizeof(Generator)`), 2,852 B under the 16 KB `static_assert` (H18). `scripts/generative/generator_size.py` measures it with the firmware's compiler, and `check_all.py` fails until this paragraph states it. Perform's two blocks take about 26 K, from the fast heap when it has a span that holds each, else SDRAM (#388). The capacities are sized by construction for the largest roster, Detroit techno's and House's (Drums, Bass, Pad, Chords, Lead, FX), from one roster table: a 4-note voicing on Chords and Pad, one note on Bass and Lead. The parts:

- **Event buffer**, 10,072 B: 629 events of 16 B, with the buffer's keep priorities in spare bits of each event. It holds a step plus the step still playing, each with every note's hits, a note-off for each hit inside the step, and a note-off for every note already sounding. Only one of the two can be a bar line, so the re-strikes of late entry and the Section-line cut are counted once, and only the step being decided can hold Control events (each control each control set can move, 108 a step), since they play on its grid line. A Sound event per part fits in each of the two steps.
- **Part state**, 1,464 B: 6 parts of 244 B. A part holds its Loop or Motif line (2 B a step) and where its last Loop note started (for Avoid's plain gate, §5.5), each lane's pick with the bar it began on and room for a pick that waits out a freeze, its Answer and freeze state, its lane gates as a bitmask, and its held voicing (4 pitches and the size, with the Density state of each note place and the mute state in one byte).
- **Control sets**, 480 B: 20 sets of 24 B. A Drums part has one per lane as well as its kit-wide one. Each set keeps its Throw state and its drift walk's points, so a step draws only when the walk passes a point, and whether it has a delay setup with the delay Throw's level last sent (§11.4).
- **Sounding-note and late-entry tables**, 428 B each: one pool of 35 notes of 12 B, each part given what the buffer counts for its role.
- **Pending list**, 400 B: 33 commands of 12 B, one slot each for a key change, a force or End, and a transition, and a take, a mute, a solo, a sound and an octave shift slot per part (a `static_assert` in `generator.h` holds it).
- **Arc**, 76 B, with a hold that waits for a new roster's Groove. The other 184 B are the Generator's own fields: the Recipe without its sound picks (76 B, with each part's octave shift and its Part bias per macro, 48 B), the takes, the voiced chord and the walk's state, the fill state (with a held Roll's steps), the drop-out state, Respond's busier halves (§5.5), the instance's start step, each part's last landed sound and each part's macro-layer Space for a stopped instance's Throws (a Part bias moves it per part).

Outside the block, all in SDRAM (`PLACE_SDRAM_BSS`), so the sound picks add no internal SRAM:
- The Current Recipe, 14,016 B: the Recipe, its sound picks and the copy a genre pick stages (2.7 K each), two palette overrides (4.1 K each), one in use and one staged, and the name of its Recipe file (98 B).
- Recipe SAVE's copy of the Current Recipe, 2,768 B: what a save writes, taken before its first card access ([§16.4](16-recipe-files.md#164-save)).
- The sound controls' bound params for each piece (where each sits, its home and range), 14,180 B: 12.7 K for 20 control sets and 1.4 K for the outgoing roster's Space params during a transition's Throws. It is sized from the [§11.2](11-sound-controls.md#112-mapping-per-engine) table for every control set moving every param it could, and finds a patch cable by its source and destination, not a pointer.
- Sound re-rolls' three sets of sound picks, 8 K: the picks a preload rolls, those of each part's sound waiting for its line, and each part's previous sound.
- Transitions: the pending Recipe and its sound picks (2.7 K), the roster a preload builds, and up to 24 retiring Outputs' handles twice over (the list and the free job's, 2.0 K and 1.8 K). The handles keep each part's control ranges ([§11.1](11-sound-controls.md#111-the-six-sound-controls), 24 B of a part's 76 B), which freeing never reads: 1.1 K in all, kept so that a retiring Output is the same `PartOutput` the roster held.

Perform's adapter is in internal RAM (`.data`): the playing roster and the outgoing one, 456 B each, of which the control ranges are 144 B.

**H1, measured** on hardware over the four Techno subgenres (Acid, Melodic, Dub and Detroit techno; Release test-port builds, the Perform scripts of each subgenre's session; a 33.33 MHz OSTM count around each step decision in the tick, reported every 64 steps as `gen_h1`): a step decision costs 60–69 µs on average per session, at most 212–246 µs in a session. That is the "tens of µs" assumed, on average, so the phrase-line fallback of [§12.2](12-runtime.md#122-when-steps-are-decided) isn't needed. Re-measure as genres add parts and layers: each M2 genre's slice records H1 and H16 (with `culled=`) for its genre from its own Perform session script, in its one final hardware run, and H9 when its roster is the largest yet. Where the step's code lies across cache lines moves the figure by about 2 %: `docs/generative/implementation-notes/runner.md` says how it is pinned.

**H2, measured** on hardware with Techno's first sound-controls roster (Release test-port build, `scripts/generative/perform_sound_controls_h2.txt`, reported every 4 bars as `gen_h2`), over four sessions on one Deluge; a piece binds 27–28 params over 5 control sets (the Drums Kit's affect-entire params, the acid Bass, three FX rows). In the Intro, where Energy rises and Brightness, Decay, Drive and Space move together on 15–16 steps in every 4 bars, applying a step's Control events costs 25–27 µs on average and at most 32 µs in the tick, for about 20 params. The Cortex-A9 divides 64-bit values in software: with two such divisions per param, the same apply cost 33 µs. The ramps' render cost was found by playing the same piece twice, with the test port's `hold ramps` dropping every Control event the second time: per 128 samples, the Intro renders 80–119 µs slower with ramps (10–14%). A Break, where the sound stays shaded with nothing ramping, renders 62 µs slower (9%), so most of that is the shaded sound (longer releases), and the ramps themselves cost about 20–60 µs per 128 samples, 1–2% of the CPU. A Groove at home renders the same within 1 µs, so a param left flagged as interpolating after its ramp ends costs nothing measurable. The slowest render was 0.41 ms either way. The worst case, every bound param rewritten on every step (the test port's `hold stress`: all 28 params ramping halfway up and down their ranges in turn), costs 22 µs a step in the tick and about 280 µs per 128 samples in the render against the same piece with no ramps, in the Intro and the Groove alike: about 10 µs per 128 samples, 0.35% of the CPU, for each param ramping all the time, with the slowest render at 0.59 ms. So at the macro layer's pace the ramps cost little, and no phrase-line or coarser grain is needed. Budget about 0.35% of the CPU per param a layer keeps ramping.

**Drift and gestures, measured** on hardware with Techno's roster before the subgenres (Release test-port build, one load, `perform_sound_controls_h2.txt`, `perform_skeleton.txt`, `perform_transition.txt`). Techno bound 12 control sets and 60–63 params. In a Groove at the default macros only drift moves: 6.3–7.5 params armed a step, and the render costs 65–90 µs more per 128 samples than the same piece under `hold ramps` (about 2.2–3.1% CPU, 0.35–0.41% per param that keeps ramping). The Intro costs 127–130 µs more and a Break's first windows 109–131 µs. Applying a step's Control events takes 13–20 µs on average, 92 µs at most on the Intro's first step. No render culled a voice (`gen_h16 culls=0`), and the slowest render in any window was 617 µs.

**H9, measured** on hardware (Release, the `gen_entry done` event; entry as a modal runner job, timed from the OK press to Perform's song swapped in) with Acid techno's roster, over the blank song leaving lands on: 129–172 ms over two runs. Every entry reads its samples from the card: leaving empties the sample cache (below). Detroit techno's entry, with six parts, while the heavy song Ttt plays (#251, `perform_detroit_heavy.txt`, Release test-port build at e25cbe583): 190 ms. Acid techno's entries in the same session took 150–197 ms, three of them over a loaded Ttt (`perform_heap_cycle_heavy.txt`) 153–175 ms. BACK 5 ms into a cold build cancels it, and the job has unwound 12 ms after the OK. Leaving takes 16–17 ms, 0.7–1.2 ms of it (`gen_left`'s `delete_us`, measured before #136 moved its start to the undo log's clear, which Perform keeps empty) deleting Perform's song and every unused cached sample. With a large sample cache it is no slower: after loading Ttt (`perform_leave_heavy.txt`: 3.4 MB of Ttt's samples still cached when Perform's entry deletes the song), leaving took 16 ms, 1.5 ms of it deleting. At e25cbe583 (#251) every leave took 17–18 ms and `delete_us` was 1.2–2.0 ms: 1,462 µs leaving Detroit techno's six parts with Ttt's samples cached, 1,675–1,773 µs after Ttt with Acid, 1,995 µs at most. That is under 3 ms, so the delete stays one piece.

**H16 and the transition, measured** on hardware with Techno's roster before the subgenres (Release test-port build, one load, `perform_transition.txt` and `perform_transition_input.txt`). No render culled a voice in any 4-bar window of the session (`gen_h16 culls=0`), around each transition's Drop, seam and cut line included, so neither Kick nor Bass was culled; the slowest render in a window was 193–422 µs (no late renders). A Regenerate's preload (roll, every sound re-rolled away from the playing one, three Outputs built and linked) took 178–224 ms; a BACK tap cancelled one 115 ms in. During preloads every press was taken within 9 ms of being due (`input_lag`), and gold-knob edges kept their 160 ms pace (no card wait held one back). A retiring Output still sounding at its cap faded over a beat and was freed. Over each enter, Regenerate and leave cycle both heaps came back to the byte. The start step's work in the tick (the roster swap, the Current Recipe, binding the sound controls; `gen_transition start ... us=`, Release test-port build) takes 125–130 µs on average per session, single start steps 96–142 µs. Most of it is cache misses in two copies of cold SDRAM data; `docs/generative/implementation-notes/runner.md` says why and what keeps it from growing.

**H13 in part** (#143, Release test-port build, `perform_steering_log.txt` and eight other Perform scripts on hardware, 0 failed): a piece header is 6,471–6,498 B for Acid techno's roster (two synths, two Kits with 15 rows) and up to 7,536 B for Detroit techno's six parts; writing it in the tick, at the piece's step 0 decision, costs 99–152 µs once per piece (outside H1's timing). Busy steering (both macros turned a detent about every 16th) logged 260 records in 272 steps, 1,308 B: about 5 B a step. So a 5-minute piece at 128 BPM (2,560 steps) takes 6.5–7.5 KB of header plus up to about 13 KB of steering at that rate: 128 KB holds about 6 such pieces steered on every step, and about 9–10 steered on half the steps (`steering_log_spec` pins 10 for a 6 KB header and a turn every other step). Steering on every step costs more than a header; a quiet piece costs its header alone. H1 in the same session: 62–107 µs mean per 64 steps, at most 285 µs; H16: no culls. The full check, 10 marked pieces on hardware, waits on #144's marks.

**H1 and H16 over every genre** (#132), measured in the same session (`perform_genre_ring.txt`, 103 expects, 0 failed: all eight genres in one boot, each change style at least once). H1: every window's mean stayed under 150 µs, the highest 107.4 µs (Psytrance's Outro); House's 99.6 µs, at most 289 µs, with 15 voices and a slowest render of 1,142 µs. H16: one window culled the Drone twice, in Psytrance's Outro at its change into Dub; no window culled DRUMS or BASS. SDRAM: the internal heap's free room went down to 487,736 B at the seams, where the incoming roster loads beside the outgoing one, and the external heap was back to 2,052,032 B by the end.

**H1 after a 20:00 Compose song** (#388), measured on hardware (test-port builds at 67e7a7b7d and 13edf3fa1, `perform_after_compose.txt`, 64 expects, 0 failed each; the heap and H1 figures below are 13edf3fa1's). Acid techno is entered twice on the same pinned roll. The first entry is from a blank song. The second comes after a 20:00 Psytrance Compose song (752 bars, 1,032 K of general heap), saved in 372 ms and loaded back in 228 ms with the built song still current. That is the state H19 left. Each entry plays six 64-step `gen_h1` windows: two over the Intro (bars 0–8) and four over a forced Groove (bars 8–24). Perform entry allocates its two Generator blocks with `allocMaxSpeed` after building its song, while the old song is still loaded (`gen_generator_heap`). From the blank song both blocks landed in internal RAM. After the loaded-back Compose song both landed in SDRAM. The cause is fragmentation, not a lack of free bytes. 97,504 B of internal heap was free before the blocks, and the two blocks take about 26 K. But a 13,396 B block is past the 128 B small-object size (`kInternalSwitchSize`), so it needs one free span of at least that size in the internal general region (`MEMORY_REGION_INTERNAL`). `freeInternal()` also counts the small-object region and every fragment. The largest free span in the internal general region was 224 B (`gen_generator_heap largest_span=`). From the blank song it was 565,880 B, with 681,272 B free. In SDRAM, H1 rose by about 10%: the Groove windows' means went from 64.3–65.2 µs to 70.1–71.7 µs, and the Intro windows' from 73.7–75.3 µs to 78.9–80.8 µs. The maximum was 169 µs in both entries, no voice was culled, and that is well inside the 150 µs stop limit. Leaving brought the internal heap back to 850 K. If the speed matters later, two fixes could keep the blocks in internal RAM: allocate them before the old song's structures, or free the old song first (#388, the owner's call). Either needs a span the blocks fit in, not just free bytes.

**H17, measured** on hardware (Release test-port build, b395ec5d0; Techno, `perform_midi.txt`, MIDI follow's default mappings, feedback at the HIGH rate, 40 ms). Feedback sends a value only when it moved since it was last sent (§13.7), so while a piece played with ACID focused the steady windows sent 361–412 B a second on DIN's 3 B a CC, 12–13 % of the 3125 B/s link, at most 7 CCs a pass; later windows sent 14–109 B/s. A context change (a focus, entry) sends every mapped param and the macros in one pass, 84 CCs (252 B): its window sent 600 B/s, as stock sends one. delugemu gave 431–568 B/s, at most 8 CCs a pass, the same with every sound-control param re-ramped each step (`hold stress`). Stock's own automation feedback re-sends every automated param each pass, unfiltered. The cost of sending only moved values: a controller that drops a message, or is plugged in mid-set, shows a stale value until the next context change.

**Leaving frees what entry built.** After an enter/leave cycle the internal and external heaps are exactly where they were before entry. The first cycle after boot is the exception: it costs 320 B of internal heap once (the sample cache's bookkeeping), and on hardware it leaves 296 B more external heap free than before, since that leave also deletes the samples cached before entry. A loaded song doesn't change this (#251, `perform_heap_cycle_heavy.txt`): Ttt loaded, Perform entered and left, three times on one boot, left the internal heap at 847,504 B after every leave, and external within 8 B (2,051,936–2,051,944 B), as did the session's later leaves over the blank song (847,504–847,512 B). So the 2,328 B M1 saw after leaving Perform entered from Ttt wasn't a leak. Two rules give this:
- Leaving deletes every audio file no song uses, before it builds the blank song: the samples Perform loaded, and the previous song's cached samples too. Stock firmware keeps unused samples as a stealable cache, which still holds heap, and each entry picks new sounds. The cost is card reads: entering again, or loading the old song back, reads its samples from the card.
- Building the blank song leaks nothing: a stock Browser leak lost 96–104 B of external heap on every SHIFT+LOAD → NEW (`docs/upstream-candidates.md`).

**Raising H18's limit** (the user's rule). The 16 KB `static_assert` is a tripwire, not a hardware limit: Perform
allocates two Generator blocks from the fast heap first, SDRAM when it has no room. A change that doesn't fit first audits the block for wasted space
and fixes what it finds: field sizes, padding, capacities above their derivation, and stored state that could be
derived. Only then does it raise the limit, by 4 KB at a time, updating the H18 row and the size breakdown above, and
its PR reports the audit.
